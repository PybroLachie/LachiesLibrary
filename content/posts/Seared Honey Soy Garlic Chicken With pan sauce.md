# Notes
- **serves:** the amount of serves in this recipe refers to the amount of units of cooked and flavoured chicken that is produced, rather than a specific amount of people it would feed. depending on your individual protein needs & the amount of sides you are accompanying the chicken with, you may need more or less than a single serve. each serve is approximately **94 grams** of cooked chicken (accounting for 25% of the chickens weight being lost in the cooking process)
- **Cooking Surface:** While I do highly recommend a stainless steel pan for this recipe, it is not a hard requirement. The stainless steel helps get a nice sear on the chicken and is very easy to extract the [fond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fond) from, however I have cooked this recipe in a non stick ceramic pan with very similar results, I used both a stainless pan and a non stick pan when taking photos for this recipe, you will see the differences are minimal.
- **High smoke point oil:** This is a huge life saver if cooking in stainless steel for getting a great sear without smoking your kitchen out, I have gotten away with using extra virgin olive oil under a range hood on maximum strength before, but I would recommend just picking up a high smoke point oil such as avocado oil.
# Info:

|                                                |                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Yield:                                         | 8 serves of chicken |
| Prep Time                                      | 25 minutes          |
| Cook Time (searing chicken and building sauce) | 30 minutes          |
# Equipment Required
* Stove
* 2 large (28cm diameter or larger) pans (stainless steel recommended) 
* 2 lids that fit your pans 
* 1 Mixing spoon
* 1 chopping board
* 1 large kitchen knife
* 1 glass of water (for temperature checking)
* instant read kitchen food thermometer 
* 2 cup measuring cup / medium bowel
* 1 small bowel / cup
# Ingredients
- 1kg chicken breast (4 pieces)
- 1/2 cup soy sauce
- 1/8 - 1/4 cup honey depending on your sweetness preference
- 4-6 gloves of garlic
- Desired sides (This chicken works excellently in rolls or raps with cheese and lettuce, or served with jasmine rice and broccolini)
- 2 tbsp cornflour
- avocado oil / high smoke point oil
# Method - Prep
1. Peel and roughly chop garlic (if you microwave the garlic cloves for about 15 seconds they will be much easier to peel, but be careful as this can make them quite hot)
2. add garlic to a 2 cup measuring jug / medium bowel
3. add soy sauce & honey to garlic, then add water until you have about 2 cups of liquid in the cup / bowel, mix well with a spoon until you can no longer feel the honey dragging on the bottom   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010502927.png)
4. in a small bowel / cup mix together cornflour and 2 tbsp water mix until a smooth slurry is created (you can add more water if needed)   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010514632.png)
5. remove chicken breasts from packaging and slice each one in half horizontally to create 8 thin slices of chicken (try to keep chicken slices even in size as this will result in the chicken being finished cooking all at a similar time, but it will not be the end of the world if some are bigger), pat surface if chicken dry with a paper towel. ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010521741.png)
# Method - Cooking
1. preheat both pans dry (no oil) on medium-high heat for about 3-5 minutes (if using stainless steel, heat until the [Leiden frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidenfrost_effect) effect is achieved (test by pouring a small amount of water in the pan, if the water beads up and moves around the pan it is ready))   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010552520.png)
2. add oil to both pans and allow about 15 seconds for the oil to absorb heat before adding 4 chicken pieces to each pan (if cooking on stainless steel be aware that the chicken will stick to the pan when you place it, do not panic, it will release on its own once it has a nice sear)
3. cook chicken for 5-6 minutes **per side** and check temperature   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010605833.png)
4. if the chicken has not reached at least 74 degrees C in all areas then add about half a cup of water to each pan and cover with lids, allow to steam for 5 minutes at a time checking temperature in-between until the entire chicken breast has reached at least 74 degrees C (This will not ruin your sear, it will just help heat the chicken from both sides without drying it out too much)   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010614679.png)
5. temporarily turn of heat on stove and remove chicken to a clean large plate, cover in foil.   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010626028.png)   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010640875.png)
6. if your pan got hot enough, you should see some beautiful golden brown colour on your chicken, and a wonderful layer of chicken fond on the bottom of the pans (this is not a burnt pan, this is a gold mine of chicken flavour)   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010703257.png)
7. after the pans have had at least 30 seconds to cool, set heat to low and give the soy sauce mixture a quick mix before adding just enough to cover the surface of each pan (do not add it all yet)
8. slowly stir the mixture and lightly scrape over the bits of stuck on chicken to release them into the sauce (this is not a matter of strength, if the bits do not scrape of the bottom of the pan, increase the heat slightly and keep stirring over it in passes)
9. once most / all of the chicken fond is released from the bottom of the pans, add the rest of the soy sauce mixture and increase heat to medium or until a slight simmer is achieved   ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010759852.png)
10. allow sauce to simmer with frequent stirring until reduced by about 30-50%, taste sauce occasionally with a tea spoon (remember, a good sauce should taste a little *too* strong, as it's flavour will be naturally diluted by what it is served on) (if you feel that the sauce tastes way too strong, you can add more water and mix it in and allow it to reduce down again)
11. once you are happy with the flavour of the sauce, give your cornflour slurry a quick mix and add 2 tablespoons to each pan and mix in well, allow this to simmer with frequent stirring and the sauce will thicken up in about 1-2 minutes.
12. once you are happy with the sauce thickness, reduce heat back to low and add the chicken back into the pans (along with any juices that collected on the plate during resting)
13. turn and coat chicken in sauce, serve immediately with scopes of extra sauce over the chicken.    ![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010838405.png)
# Serving
This chicken works excellently both in a roll, wrap, or between bread with some cheese and lettuce, or with rice and steamed broccolini (if serving with broccoli, throw it in the pan with the sauce after steaming for 10-15 seconds to soak up that chicken flavour)

# Storage
Leftovers store excellently in plastic bags / Tupperware with a couple scopes of sauce to keep the moisture high.
![Image Description](/LachiesLibrary/images/Seared%20Honey%20Soy%20Garlic%20Chicken%20With%20pan%20sauce-20261103010939871.png)
# Cleaning
an awesome side effect of making a pan sauce, because the process removes almost all fond from the bottom of the pan, they clean extremely easily!
