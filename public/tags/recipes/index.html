Seared Honey Soy Garlic Chicken With pan sauce

Notes serves: the amount of serves in this recipe refers to the amount of units of cooked and flavoured chicken that is produced, rather than a specific amount of people it would feed. depending on your individual protein needs & the amount of sides you are accompanying the chicken with, you may need more or less than a single serve. each serve is approximately 94 grams of cooked chicken (accounting for 25% of the chickens weight being lost in the cooking process) Cooking Surface: While I do highly recommend a stainless steel pan for this recipe, it is not a hard requirement. The stainless steel helps get a nice sear on the chicken and is very easy to extract the fond from, however I have cooked this recipe in a non stick ceramic pan with very similar results, I used both a stainless pan and a non stick pan when taking photos for this recipe, you will see the differences are minimal. High smoke point oil: This is a huge life saver if cooking in stainless steel for getting a great sear without smoking your kitchen out, I have gotten away with using extra virgin olive oil under a range hood on maximum strength before, but I would recommend just picking up a high smoke point oil such as avocado oil. Info: Yield: 8 serves of chicken Prep Time 25 minutes Cook Time (searing chicken and building sauce) 30 minutes Equipment Required Stove 2 large (28cm diameter or larger) pans (stainless steel recommended) 2 lids that fit your pans 1 Mixing spoon 1 chopping board 1 large kitchen knife 1 glass of water (for temperature checking) instant read kitchen food thermometer 2 cup measuring cup / medium bowel 1 small bowel / cup Ingredients 1kg chicken breast (4 pieces) 1/2 cup soy sauce 1/8 - 1/4 cup honey depending on your sweetness preference 4-6 gloves of garlic Desired sides (This chicken works excellently in rolls or raps with cheese and lettuce, or served with jasmine rice and broccolini) 2 tbsp cornflour avocado oil / high smoke point oil Method - Prep Peel and roughly chop garlic (if you microwave the garlic cloves for about 15 seconds they will be much easier to peel, but be careful as this can make them quite hot) add garlic to a 2 cup measuring jug / medium bowel add soy sauce & honey to garlic, then add water until you have about 2 cups of liquid in the cup / bowel, mix well with a spoon until you can no longer feel the honey dragging on the bottom in a small bowel / cup mix together cornflour and 2 tbsp water mix until a smooth slurry is created (you can add more water if needed) remove chicken breasts from packaging and slice each one in half horizontally to create 8 thin slices of chicken (try to keep chicken slices even in size as this will result in the chicken being finished cooking all at a similar time, but it will not be the end of the world if some are bigger), pat surface if chicken dry with a paper towel. Method - Cooking preheat both pans dry (no oil) on medium-high heat for about 3-5 minutes (if using stainless steel, heat until the Leiden frost effect is achieved (test by pouring a small amount of water in the pan, if the water beads up and moves around the pan it is ready)) add oil to both pans and allow about 15 seconds for the oil to absorb heat before adding 4 chicken pieces to each pan (if cooking on stainless steel be aware that the chicken will stick to the pan when you place it, do not panic, it will release on its own once it has a nice sear) cook chicken for 5-6 minutes per side and check temperature if the chicken has not reached at least 74 degrees C in all areas then add about half a cup of water to each pan and cover with lids, allow to steam for 5 minutes at a time checking temperature in-between until the entire chicken breast has reached at least 74 degrees C (This will not ruin your sear, it will just help heat the chicken from both sides without drying it out too much) temporarily turn of heat on stove and remove chicken to a clean large plate, cover in foil. if your pan got hot enough, you should see some beautiful golden brown colour on your chicken, and a wonderful layer of chicken fond on the bottom of the pans (this is not a burnt pan, this is a gold mine of chicken flavour) after the pans have had at least 30 seconds to cool, set heat to low and give the soy sauce mixture a quick mix before adding just enough to cover the surface of each pan (do not add it all yet) slowly stir the mixture and lightly scrape over the bits of stuck on chicken to release them into the sauce (this is not a matter of strength, if the bits do not scrape of the bottom of the pan, increase the heat slightly and keep stirring over it in passes) once most / all of the chicken fond is released from the bottom of the pans, add the rest of the soy sauce mixture and increase heat to medium or until a slight simmer is achieved allow sauce to simmer with frequent stirring until reduced by about 30-50%, taste sauce occasionally with a tea spoon (remember, a good sauce should taste a little too strong, as it’s flavour will be naturally diluted by what it is served on) (if you feel that the sauce tastes way too strong, you can add more water and mix it in and allow it to reduce down again) once you are happy with the flavour of the sauce, give your cornflour slurry a quick mix and add 2 tablespoons to each pan and mix in well, allow this to simmer with frequent stirring and the sauce will thicken up in about 1-2 minutes. once you are happy with the sauce thickness, reduce heat back to low and add the chicken back into the pans (along with any juices that collected on the plate during resting) turn and coat chicken in sauce, serve immediately with scopes of extra sauce over the chicken. Serving This chicken works excellently both in a roll, wrap, or between bread with some cheese and lettuce, or with rice and steamed broccolini (if serving with broccoli, throw it in the pan with the sauce after steaming for 10-15 seconds to soak up that chicken flavour) ...

Cheesymite Scrolls Recipe

Info: Yield: 48 Scrolls Prep time 20 minutes Cook time (pressing dough and cooking) 20-25 minutes Equipment Required: Oven several chopping boards / large plates Ingredients: 6 sheets of puff pastry 600g grated tasty cheese half a jar of vegemite 2 egg yolks Method preheat oven to 240 degrees Celsius take pastry out of the freezer about 5 minutes before use to allow the sheets to thaw a little. lay the sheets out across cutting boards / plates. if the sheets are still frozen together, use the edge of a sharp knife to leverage them apart. coat each sheet in a thin layer of vegemite, remember that each scroll will end up with several layers of this coating, so don’t put it on too strong. sprinkle cheese over each covered sheet of pastry. roll into logs, using the egg yolk to seal them together. cover each log in the remaining egg yolk cut each log into 8 slices. place slices on their side on a baking tray/s bake for 20-25 minutes or until golden brown Allow to rest and firm up outside of the oven for about 5 minutes before transferring to plates for serving

Pizza Dough Recipie

Info: Yield: 3 pizza bases Cook time (dough making and rising) 90 minutes Cook time (pressing dough and cooking) 30 minutes Equipment Required: Mixer with dough hooks 3 stainless steel or ceramic bowls plastic wrap Ingredients: 1 cup of warm water (about 40 degrees Celsius) 8 grams of dry yeast 2 teaspoons of sugar 1/2 teaspoon of salt 3 1/2 cups of plain flour 5 tablespoons of olive oil for cooking Method - creating the dough Place water, yeast, sugar, salt, and 1 cup of flour in mixing bowl, mix on lowest setting until just combined. add 3 tablespoons of olive oil and 1 cup of flour, mix on medium/high for 30 seconds add 1 cup of flour, mix on medium/high for 8 minutes (giving your mixer breaks based on your instruction manual). During or after kneading your dough in the mixer, line up 3 bowls and use the remaining olive oil to oil up the inside of the bowls (the measurement does not need to be exact! just get a light coating on everything). take your finished large ball of dough and weight on digital scale, divide into 3 roughly even portions (a few grams of is fine). the easiest way to separate the dough is to grab 2 sections with your hands and twist, you can also cut it with a knife. it is okay if you end up needing to tear some more dough of a ball to get it to get all your portions to the right weight, you can lightly knead this back together before the dough rises. place the 3 divided dough balls into the 3 oiled bowls, use the ball to rub the outside of the bowl, try to get the entire ball covered in oil, cover the bowls snugly with plastic wrap. leave to rest for ATLEAST 1 hours. if you like, you can put it in the fridge for 24 hours for a slow rise, the resulting dough is a bit nicer- but not by a huge amount. Method - working with the dough preheat oven to 230 degrees Celsius. line a pizza tray with baking paper. take large cutting board and lightly coat with some of the remaining flour. take a single ball of dough out of a bowl and place on the cutting board, sprinkle with some flour. if you find the dough hard to work with, you can dust your hands with flour as well to make the slippery dough easier to handle. press the dough down into a disc shape, adding more flour if needed. transfer the dough disc from the cutting board to the lined pizza tray, continue to press down until reaching desired size (I like a thin crust and often push mine right to the edges of a large tray to achieve this). add your pizza sauce, toppings, and cheese. repeat for remaining dough balls. cook time varies based on pizza toppings, but typically a cook time of 12-20 minutes results in a beautiful crust and a light fluffy base. Notes: for an extra crunchy crust, you can add a light brushing of olive oil to the outer rim of your pizza
